# Key Insights — Pricing & Inventory Optimization

## 1. Theoretical Revenue Often Overestimates Real Revenue

Revenue simulations based purely on demand modeling suggest higher achievable revenue.
However, once **inventory constraints are applied**, the feasible revenue decreases significantly.

This highlights the gap between **theoretical demand and operational reality**.

## 2. Deep Discounts Create High Stockout Risk

Price reductions significantly increase demand, particularly for elastic categories.
However, without corresponding inventory adjustments, these discounts result in **early stockouts and lost sales opportunities**.

Aggressive pricing strategies therefore introduce **operational risk**.

## 3. Moderate Pricing Strategies Deliver the Most Reliable Outcomes

Moderate price changes produce a balanced outcome:

* Stable demand
* Manageable inventory requirements
* Reduced stockout risk

These strategies maximize **realized revenue rather than theoretical revenue potential**.

## 4. Price Elasticity Varies by Category

Elasticity analysis shows that certain categories respond strongly to price changes, while others remain relatively stable.

Elastic categories benefit from controlled discounting, whereas inelastic categories generate revenue without aggressive pricing changes.

## 5. Inventory Planning Must Align With Pricing Decisions

Pricing strategies cannot be executed in isolation.
Promotional campaigns must be coordinated with **inventory availability and replenishment cycles** to avoid fulfillment failures.

---

# Executive Recommendation

Pricing optimization should be implemented alongside **inventory-aware planning**.

Companies should:

• Align promotions with inventory readiness
• Avoid deep discounts without stock buildup
• Focus on strategies that maximize **realized revenue rather than theoretical demand**
